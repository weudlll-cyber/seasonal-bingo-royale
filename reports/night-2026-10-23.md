### Piece 5 — 200-player screens

**Cause, measured** (simulated 200-player royale, one third through, the server's own `liveView` and `JSON.stringify`): every screen's delta fetch after a draw carried 175 KiB, of which 169 KiB was the player list (every player's first-round card and standing), unchanged since the first round. 200 screens per draw made 34 MiB of JSON and about 0.3 s of serialising on the event loop per draw, before HTTP. A delta with a later round start carried 243 KiB (76 KiB of the event with every card, 167 KiB of the list again). A gap in the client (frames arriving while a fetch ran) reloaded the whole log: 522 KiB per screen at that point (the load script never did this, browsers would).

**Fix**: a delta leaves out the player list unless a join, the first round's start or the finish is among its events (later cards come in their `roundStarted` events); the client keeps the list it holds; any frame, a gap included, fetches only the events after the held seq (a reconnect still reloads everything). A delta of draws and taps is now under 20 KB.

### Piece 6 — reliable tests and the final check

**Why six timing tests failed under the full run (NIGHT-3)**: they measure with browser timers (speed window, missed-number delay, timed draws) while three heavy browsers ran beside them on one machine, so their timers fired late. **Fix**: these specs (`missedNumbers`, `drawControl`, `manual`, `newRun`) run in their own Playwright projects after all other specs, one test at a time and one browser after another (`workers: 1`, chained `dependencies`). No limit of the game was changed.

Two more causes found on the way, both fixed:

- **Leaked browser contexts** (first full run tonight: three WebKit tests timed out, simple ones took minutes, always the last ones in a worker). Specs that open their own contexts never closed them; their pages kept sockets and 4-per-second timers running for the rest of the worker's life. Now `closeContexts` runs after every test of those six specs.
- **Vitest starved its own jsdom tests** (a test that takes 0.3 s alone took over 5 s inside `pnpm verify`). Vitest now uses half the cores (slowest web test 3.8 s at 13 workers, 2.6 s at 7), the web project's per-test timeout is 20 s, the CPU-budget test and the D17 integration tests have their own budgets.

## FOR THE OWNER

**How starting works now.** In Host a match, choose under Start: "I start it from the console" (as before), "At a set time" or "When every place is taken". A set time is now an announcement: you can always press Start match, before or after it. Tick "Start automatically at the start time" (off unless you tick it) and the match starts itself at that time when at least 2 players have joined: the 10-second countdown begins at the time. You can tick or untick it in the console until the match starts. If fewer than 2 players are there at the time, nothing starts and nothing is cancelled: the console says "Not enough players at the start time — start it yourself or abort." The choice survives a server restart.

**What host and players see about registration.** The same line on your console, in the waiting room and on the lobby card: "Registration open until the host starts the match"; or "Starts at 22:25 (in 12 min) · starts automatically" / "· the host starts it", counting down live (with the day when it is not today); or "3 places left" for start when full. "Starting…" appears only in the real 10-second countdown, never minutes before.

**What a cancelled match looks like.** Host and players get their own page instead of the game screen: the match title, "This match was cancelled by the host." or "… by the system.", "Reason: …" in the words given, "Back to the lobby", and for the host "Host another match". The lobby lists it as "Cancelled" with View.

**The 200-player run.** 200 test players, one royale match, a draw every 4 seconds, every fifth player tapping: a drawn number reached the players in 27 ms typically and within 58 ms for 95 % of them; a player's screen was fully up to date within 439 ms for 95 % of them (target 1 s; it was 4.6 s). The server used about a fifth of one core on average and under 500 MB; its worst delay was 229 ms (was 1.7 s); no errors; the match finished in 4.4 min. The cause was that every screen downloaded the full player list (175 KiB at 200 players) after every draw; now it comes only when it changes. The 200 test accounts were deleted.

**Also changed on screen.** On a phone every dropdown option of the create form is readable (the options no longer repeat their heading: "Timed (every 5 seconds)", "Mark for me after 3 seconds", "Wait a few seconds"); during the pause between royale rounds the header says "stage 1 (qualification) · round 1 over · stage 2 (elimination) next". Pictures in `reports/screens/night-4/`.
